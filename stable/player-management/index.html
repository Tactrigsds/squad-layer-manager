Features

Player management

SLM helps admins moderate and balance the players on their servers, from the dashboard or in game.

Teams and balancesource

The teams panel lists both teams by squad, side by side, or in one table when the panel is too narrow. A grouping mode sorts players into coloured groups by BattleMetrics flag, Discord role, a pattern on their name, or their group in a standard Squad admin list. A player's username takes their group's colour wherever it is shown. Two grouping modes cover the common needs: one for balance and one for admin purposes.

The built-in Party grouping mode groups players by the in-game party they queued with, so admins can filter, sort and select a team by party.

The Teams Breakdown chart counts each team's players by group. See Charts.

Player detailssource

A player's details window lists their IDs and profile links, their groups and BattleMetrics flags, their team and squad, and their recent chat.

A history icon beside the username marks a player who has gone by other usernames. Click it to list each username and the dates the player used it.

Use the expand button beside the activity filter to give the player's server activity the whole window. Press it again to bring back the rest of the player's details.

Admin actionssource

Admins can warn, kick, time out, kill or swap a player from the player's context menu, wherever the player's name or row appears. From the same menu, admins can also remove a player from their squad, demote a commander, and manage the player's BattleMetrics flags and notes. Disband a squad from the squad's own menu. Every player action also applies to a whole squad or to any selection of players at once.

A timeout kicks the player, and kicks them again whenever they rejoin any server SLM manages, until the timeout expires or is cancelled. /timeout Kestrel 2h spamming in command chat times Kestrel out for two hours.

On a phone, the same actions open from a menu button at the end of each player's row.

Roles decide which of these actions each admin may take, and can cap some of them, such as the longest timeout. See Permissions and access control.

Message shorthandssource

A message shorthand is a preset message for a common offence, such as teamkilling or wasting assets. Each shorthand holds one message per action, so a warn for teamkilling can read "Do not team kill." while a kick for the same offence reads "You have been kicked for team killing." The dashboard's action dialogs offer the shorthands in a list, beside a custom message. In game, a shorthand's keyword takes the place of the message: /kick Kestrel tk kicks Kestrel with the Teamkilling shorthand's kick message.

An install can require a reason for chosen actions. See Admin actions and commands.

BattleMetrics flags and notessource

A BattleMetrics flag marks a player in your organization, and a note is free text on their BattleMetrics profile. Admins use notes to brief each other: why a player was warned, or what to watch for next match. SLM sets flags and posts notes from the dashboard or in game, with /flag and /note, and signs each note with the name of the admin who wrote it. A player's notes load in their details window, newest first.

By default, SLM makes these changes with your organization's BattleMetrics token. An admin can save a personal access token, so that their flag and note changes are made as their own BattleMetrics account. See Personal BattleMetrics tokens.

Team swapssource

Admins move players, selections and whole squads between teams, either on the next map roll or at once.

  • Use Swap Next to add the players to the swaps panel. The panel lists each pending swap under the team the player is moving to, and counts how the teams will stand after the swaps. Several admins can edit the swaps at once, as with the layer queue. On the next map roll, SLM moves everyone on the list, and retries a swap that does not land.
  • Use Swap Now to move the players at once, after a confirmation.

SLM warns each player in game when they are marked for a swap, when their swap is removed, and when an admin swaps them on the spot. A swap keeps its destination across the map roll, even though the teams change sides. A player who leaves, or reaches their destination on their own, is dropped from the list. In-game commands such as /swapnext and /swapnow do the same from chat.

Counterbalancesource

Turn on Counterbalance to keep the teams the same size while admins edit the swaps. Whenever an admin queues or removes a swap, SLM picks swaps from the other team so that the teams end up as even as possible. A party is always swapped together. Counterbalance swaps carry a scale icon and a dashed outline. When players joining, leaving or switching teams unbalance the saved swaps, SLM re-picks them after a countdown.

Each server chooses who SLM may pick, such as never picking players in a group or players with a high K/D in the current match. See Teamswap counterbalance.

Switch requestssource

Players ask to switch teams themselves with /switch, or a trigger of your own, such as !switch. Unlike an admin swap, a switch request waits for room on the other team, and lasts only for the current match.

  • If the player's team has more players than the other, SLM switches them at once.
  • Otherwise they wait in line, first come first served on each side, and SLM reports each player's place.
  • The first players in the two lines trade places.
  • When a new player joins the team someone is waiting for, SLM moves the new player across and switches the waiting player into their slot.

A player leaves the line with /cancelswitch, and the line clears at every map roll. The Switch requests window shows both lines, with each player's place and the next pair to trade, and lets admins switch any player in it straight away.
